Self-regulation Simulator

Each construct processes information and, when the quality of its state passes its threshold, activates the next one. The quality can be induced by an intervention, an experience, new information or a new interpretation. When any threshold is not passed, the process stops and nothing new is learned. Only when every step works do the expected outcomes shift, and the shift is largest when all elements are optimal.

    How the simulation calculates
    • Each construct passes when its quality is at or above its threshold. The constructs are checked in order: value, perceived behavioural outcome, discrepancy, problem solving, motivation, goal setting, planning, implementation intention formation, behavioural engagement, monitoring, evaluation, learning.
    • At the first construct that does not pass, the cycle stops. The expected outcomes do not change.
    • When all twelve pass, the overall quality Q is the average of all qualities on a 0–100% scale (1 on the scale = 0%, the top of the scale = 100%). The learning strength is 40% × Q². Squaring makes optimal elements matter: with all qualities at the top, 40% of the remaining room is learned per cycle; with all just above the threshold, about 10%.
    • Expected positive outcomes move up by the learning strength times the room left to 100%; expected negative outcomes move down by the learning strength times their current level.
    • Thresholds are treated as fixed properties of the constructs. You can unlock them for simulation purposes; they apply to every behaviour.
